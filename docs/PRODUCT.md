# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Primary visitors are:
- **Malaysian consumers** who have heard about online scams and want a lightweight way to protect their own bank transfers.
- **Adult children** looking for a simple guardian-style tool to protect parents who are targeted by scammers.
- **Hackathon judges and technical evaluators** who need to quickly understand what Sentinel is, how it works, and why it is a viable fintech risk product.

They arrive with low attention and some anxiety about scams. They need clarity first — what is this, why should I trust it, and how do I try it — before any detail.

## Product Purpose

Sentinel Scam Shield is a browser extension that warns people **before** they send money to a likely scam. The website's job is to:
1. Communicate the problem and the product in one scroll.
2. Make the underlying technology (multi-agent AI screening, risk signals, compliance posture) feel understandable, not opaque.
3. Drive visitors to install the extension or try the live demo bank.
4. Present the business case and architecture as credible, judge-ready evidence.

Success means a visitor can land on the site, understand the product, and try it within 60 seconds.

## Brand Personality

**Simple, Exciting, Technical.**

- **Simple:** Every explanation — business case, architecture, pricing — is stripped to what matters. No jargon walls, no abstract claims.
- **Exciting:** The product prevents real money loss. The site should feel like a confident guardrail, not a dry compliance brochure.
- **Technical:** The build is real (multi-agent AI, deterministic fusion, cross-bank memory). The site surfaces that depth honestly, without hiding behind marketing fluff.

Emotional goal: “OK, I know what is happening.” Visitors should feel informed and in control, not sold to or frightened.

## Anti-references

- **Enterprise fintech dashboards.** Dense grids, tiny type, and navy-and-gold “trust” palettes feel distant and overbuilt for a consumer extension.
- **Alarmist cybersecurity sites.** Red-black urgency, skull icons, and fear-driven copy undermine the simple, confident tone.
- **Generic SaaS landing pages.** Cream backgrounds, identical icon-cards, gradient-text heroes, and “ABOUT / PROCESS / PRICING” eyebrows make the product interchangeable.
- **Over-polished startup pages.** Too much motion, too many floating gradients, or copy that promises more than the product delivers hurt credibility with judges.

## Design Principles

1. **Explain first, decorate never.** Every visual choice must help the visitor understand the product faster. If an element does not clarify, remove it.
2. **Show the real engine.** Architecture and business case are visible, not buried in docs. Technical depth is a feature, not an afterthought.
3. **One idea per section.** The landing page, business case, and architecture pages each make a single claim and prove it.
4. **Confidence without shouting.** Bold typography and clear color signal urgency, but the voice stays calm and factual.
5. **Frictionless trial.** The path from “what is this?” to “try the demo” or “install the extension” is always one click away.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Best-effort accessibility for the hackathon build:
- Sufficient color contrast for body text and interactive elements.
- Keyboard-navigable interactive elements.
- Reduced-motion alternatives for any motion added to the site.
- Clear focus indicators and readable type at all viewport sizes.
